Sheet 1

Formula Student drivetrain

EUFS 2027 car · concept

A chain-drive rear powertrain package for the university team's 2027 electric car: motor cradle, outrigger bearing, eccentric diff carriers, sprocket and half-shafts, built as a full SolidWorks assembly.

Data
Reduction13:42 (3.23)
Diff torque, design1,066 N·m (×1.5 shock)
Chain pull, design9.95 kN
Half-shaft30 × 20 mm 4340 tube
Half-shaft FoS1.98 static · 1.71 fatigue
Diff plate FoS on yield7.5

Every mount passes. The 42-tooth sprocket's window corners set the limit, at a yield FoS of 1.32 after rev B, so the next step is a thicker or solid web. This is a concept for my subteam, not the final car.

What I did
  • Mechanical powertrain engineer on Edinburgh University Formula Student (EUFS).
  • Packaged the motor cradle, outrigger, eccentric diff carriers, sprocket and half-shafts as a native SolidWorks 2026 assembly around the team's chosen motor, diff and inverter.
  • Hand calcs for shafts, splines, bolts and bearings; CalculiX FEA of the plates and sprocket.
Sheet 2

Hall-effect thruster

700 W, krypton · Endeavour

A Hall thruster for Project Xenith, the Edinburgh University rocketry society's plan to fire a student-built thruster at Race 2 Space. Boron nitride channel, iron magnetic circuit with five coils, heaterless hollow cathode.

Data
Power700 W
PropellantKrypton
ChannelOD 68 / ID 48 mm
Peak radial field200 G
Field model vs analytic−0.13 %
Anode temperature, est.~665 °C

The thermal network puts the inner coil at about 400 °C, well past polyimide-insulated wire, so it needs ceramic-insulated wire. Hardware tests come next: cold flow, field mapping and the cathode.

What I did
  • Thermal-mechanical engineer on Project Xenith, a 13-student team in Endeavour.
  • Sized the thruster from SPT scaling laws and modelled it in SolidWorks with a full bill of materials.
  • Wrote an axisymmetric magnetics FEM, checked against an analytic solenoid, and a radiation and conduction thermal network.
  • Designed the cold-flow test rig.
Sheet 3

Supersonic intake

Mach 1.6 to 3.0

An air intake that changes shape across its speed range so the engine always gets slowed, high-pressure air.

Data
Capture area200 × 300 mm
Ramps3 (2 variable)
Pressure recovery, Mach 30.833 (MIL spec 0.809)
Actuator surge load12.9 kN
Ramp 3 stress, p99136 MPa

Meets the spec at Mach 3. At Mach 2.0 and 2.4 it falls short because the cowl lip is fixed; a moving lip is the next step.

What I did
  • Set the requirements: Mach 1.6–3.0, moving ramps on ballscrews, seals, bleed plate and bypass door.
  • Worked out the shock schedule, which pushed the design from two ramps to three.
  • Built the CAD and Fusion assembly, clash-checked at every ramp position.
  • Ran SU2 CFD across the range after validating it on the Sajben case; ramp FEA and actuator hand calcs.
Sheet 4

Rocket hop vehicle

VTVL, electric pump-fed

A vertical take-off and landing test vehicle built around the aerospike in Sheet 5.

Data
Take-off mass242 kg
Thrust to weight1.62
Hop apogee101.6 m
Landing error2.0 m
Parts in Fusion45

Every structure passes with margin. The oxygen pump gets only 1.34× the suction head it needs, so it needs an inducer.

What I did
  • Chose the concept: a hop vehicle with electric pumps, built around the Sheet 5 aerospike.
  • Sized it and built a 45-part Fusion assembly, checked in SolidWorks.
  • Wrote a 3-DOF flight sim with attitude control.
  • FEA of the thrust structure, tank and legs; crosswind and feed-line CFD.
Sheet 5

Aerospike engine

5 kN, LOX / RP-1

An annular aerospike on a gimbal that steers it 6° in any direction.

Data
Thrust, sea level3.83 kN
Thrust, altitude5.0 kN
Gimbal FoS3.4
First mode66 Hz

Structurally sound, thermally not. Regen cooling can carry 0.46 MW against 0.9 MW in, so the cooling concept has to change.

What I did
  • Designed a 5 kN annular aerospike with regenerative cooling on a two-ring gimbal.
  • Gimbal kinematics and actuator sizing.
  • Plume CFD checked against Angelino's method; gimbal FEA and modal analysis; a cooling-channel optimiser.
Sheet 6

EV corner module

7075-T6

A lightweight steering knuckle and in-wheel motor mount, shaped by topology optimisation.

Data
Knuckle mass2.13 kg
Bearing L10 life8,690 h
Bump steer< 0.1°
Bearing seat49–64 °C

Geometry and kinematics are done. The final stress check is paused after the mesher failed on the organic shape.

What I did
  • Sized the knuckle and motor mount from load cases; checked bearing life and kinematics.
  • Wrote a 6-DOF suspension kinematics solver and a 3-D topology optimiser, then rebuilt the knuckle as CAD.
  • Built the 33-part Fusion corner assembly and modelled heat transfer in the coolant gallery.
